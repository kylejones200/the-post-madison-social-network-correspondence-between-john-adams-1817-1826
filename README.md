# The Post-Madison Social Network: Correspondence Between John Adams, 1817–1826

This folder contains code and resources for the Medium article:
[The Post-Madison Social Network: Correspondence Between John Adams, 1817–1826](https://medium.com/@kylejones_47003/the-post-madison-social-network-correspondence-between-john-adams-1817-1826-e20469b083a9)

## Business context

After the final term of James Madison, the American experiment continued --- in letters. From 1817 to his death in 1826, John Adams remained one of the most active voices in the country's political and philosophical life. He wrote. A lot.

Thanks to the [Founders Online Corpus](https://founders.archives.gov/), we can trace that activity, message by message. This dataset collects and standardizes over 185,000 documents from key American founders. Each record includes metadata like sender, recipient, date, and full text. Most come from the major editions --- *The Adams Papers*, *The Papers of Thomas Jefferson*, and others --- published by university presses and digitized by the U.S. National Archives.

The post-Madison slice of this corpus shows what comes after formal power. What happens when men who helped design a country step back and just talk? And who do they talk to?
